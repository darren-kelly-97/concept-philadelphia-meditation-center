We are 8 miles west of Philadelphia, between East Darby Road and Darby Road, in a row of retail shops. The Oakmont Public Parking Lot sits directly behind the Center, with the entrance on East Eagle Road.

Driving from Center City or West Philadelphia

Take Market Street west to West Chester Pike (Route 3). After crossing Township Line Road (Route 1), turn right on Darby Road. When Darby Road splits, take the left fork. Cross Eagle Road to the Oakmont Public Parking Lot on the right, directly behind PMC.

Driving from Northeast Philadelphia

Take Roosevelt Boulevard south to the Schuylkill Expressway, then the Expressway north (west) to City Avenue (Route 1). Follow Route 1 south to West Chester Pike (Route 3). Turn right onto West Chester Pike, then right onto Darby Road. When Darby Road splits, take the left fork. Cross Eagle Road to the Oakmont Public Parking Lot on the right, directly behind PMC.

By bus or train

SEPTA runs bus and train service across the Delaware Valley. Plan a trip at septa.org.

What to expect your first time

Arrive a little before the listed start time. Both chairs and cushions are available, so sit however is comfortable. No one will ask you to explain yourself, and there is no fee at the door. After most sittings there is tea and conversation, and you are welcome to stay or slip out.

On Sundays the Center opens at 6:30 PM for a 7:00 PM sit. On Wednesdays doors open at 7:00 PM, and the evening begins at 7:30 PM.

Since December 1998

Philadelphia Meditation Center opened in December 1998 and was registered as a Pennsylvania nonprofit corporation that same year, becoming a 501(c)(3) public charity in 1999 with final IRS approval in 2005. PMC was the first charity in the US to file electronically with the IRS.

The Center has a large, comfortable first floor meditation hall, and a second floor apartment with a library, kitchen, sitting room and two bedrooms. The neighborhood is friendly and quiet, with good spots nearby for walking meditation.

Dana, the practice of giving

PMC does not charge for sittings, talks, or retreats. The Center is sustained by dana, freewill donations, and by a Supporting Membership for those who want to give on a regular basis. As a 501(c)(3) public charity, donations are tax deductible.
